# Lore canon: names, places, rites and horrors from the real myths

Sources, abbreviated:
- Ap. = Apollonius Rhodius, *Argonautica*
- Apd. = Apollodorus, *Bibliotheca*
- WD = Hesiod, *Works and Days*
- P. / Ol. = Pindar, *Pythian* / *Olympian* odes
- Met. = Ovid, *Metamorphoses*
- Od. / Il. = *Odyssey* / *Iliad*

Passages were checked against Theoi, Perseus and ToposText texts on 2026-10-06.
- **≈** = standard line number from memory; the content is right, but check the line before quoting it in-game.
- **[LATE]** = the claim comes from a later source, not the ancient text.

**The art already carries this myth.** The visor phrases include SOWN MEN, THE DRAGON SLEEPS, CLASHING ROCKS, LEMNOS, HYLAS, BUTES, SIRENS, DO NOT LISTEN, ONE SANDAL, IOLCUS, MEDEA, ORPHEUS, HERACLES, SON OF HELIOS, ARGO and ARGO NAVIS. Our names extend the artist's own world; they don't replace it.

## The people: the Minyai
The crew of the Argo are called **Minyai**, "Minyans" (Ap. 1.229 ≈). This is the name for all 9,977 living Argonauts. Their home is the **Argo**: its speaking prow-beam came from the oracle oak of Dodona (Apd. 1.9.16; Ap. 1.524ff).

## Houses (oikoi)
Holder wallets become **oikoi** (households). 932 oikoi span several bloods, which is the force that cuts across factions: hearth against blood.
- A transfer moves the Argonaut to a new oikos, as an adoption or a sale into service.
- The world reacts to the transfer. Nobody steers it.

## Institutions and words
- **Boule:** the council of fixed seats.
- **Ekklesia:** the assembly.
- **Ostrakismos:** banishment voted on potsherds. Here the ballots are bone shards, with a quorum of 6,000 (Plut. *Arist.* 7 ≈).
- **Classes:** pentakosiomedimnoi, hippeis, zeugitai and **thetes**. The thetes rowed the fleet, which fits the Oarsman's Band.
- **Money:** the **obol**. Originally an iron spit; six obols make a drachma, "a handful".
- **Moral and religious vocabulary:**
  - **xenia**: guest-law
  - **miasma**: blood-pollution, a debuff
  - **katharsis**: cleansing, Circe's piglet rite (Ap. 4.700ff)
  - **hubris → nemesis → atē**: arrogance, retribution, ruin
  - **moira**: one's allotted fate
  - **kēres**: death-spirits, "swift hounds of Hades" (Ap. 4.1665ff)
- **Etymology:**
  - **Argonaut** = *Argō* + *nautēs*, "sailor"; *argos* = "shining" and "swift".
  - *Argonauta argo*, the paper nautilus: only the **females** build the shell, so "the shell is built by the mothers".
- **Traits that echo myth:**
  - Ivory cloak → Pelops's ivory shoulder; false dreams pass through the gate of ivory (Od. 19.562 ≈). The Ivory clergy deal in lies.
  - Eye Patch → the one-eyed Arimaspians who steal griffin gold (Hdt. 4.13 ≈).
  - Dragon's Breath → Hephaestus's fire-breathing bulls (Apd. 1.9.23).
  - Seafoam → Aphrodite's foam.
  - Void → "black chaos descended from heaven" (Ap. 4.1694ff).

## Horror events (sourced; all can be triggered by simulation conditions)

1. **The Lemnian Deeds** (Apd. 1.9.17; Ap. 1.793ff). A segregated faction murders its men overnight, then lies to visitors that they were exiled.
2. **Night of the Doliones** (Ap. 1.1030–77). Allies fight in the dark, mistaking each other for enemies; the next day they mourn together for three days. Queen Cleite hangs herself and the nymphs' tears become a spring.
3. **Amycus' toll** (Apd. 1.9.20). Every stranger must box the king to the death at the border.
4. **Phineus and the Harpies** (Ap. 2.178ff). A seer starves while winged thieves foul his food.
5. **The Dove Test** (Apd. 1.9.22). Something is sent through the Clashing Rocks first, as a sacrifice.
6. **Feather-bolts** (Ap. 2.1030ff). Birds shoot their feathers like arrows; the crew roof the ship with shields.
7. **Prometheus overhead** (Ap. 2.1246ff). Screams heard from the Caucasus.
8. **The ghost on the tomb** (Ap. 2.911ff ≈). Sthenelus' shade rises to look at the ship: a graveyard apparition.
9. **The hanging dead** (Ap. 3.200–209). Colchian law: men are hung in raw oxhides from trees, and only women are buried.
10. **The Hecate rite** (Ap. 3.1026ff ≈). A midnight pit and a ewe; do not turn back at footsteps or barking.
11. **The cutting of the Prometheion** (Ap. 3.844ff). Floral alchemy makes the god scream.
12. **Apsyrtus and maschalismos** (Ap. 4.464–481; Apd. 1.9.24). Jason cuts the extremities from a murdered man and spits out the blood three times, so the dead can't take revenge.
13. **The ship speaks** (Ap. 4.580ff). The beam cries out "with a human voice": a world-wide oracle broadcast.
14. **Circe's katharsis** (Ap. 4.700ff). A piglet's blood lifts miasma.
15. **The Burning Lake** (Ap. 4.596ff). Birds fall from the air; the stench is unbearable.
16. **Butes leaps** (Ap. 4.912ff). The Siren lure, which an Orpheus can block.
17. **Mopsus' death** (Ap. 4.1502ff). A snake bred from the Gorgon's blood rots his flesh; the crew circle his tomb three times.
18. **Talos** (Ap. 4.1638–88). A bronze guardian with one vein sealed by a nail; Medea's evil eye brings him down.
19. **The Pall of Darkness** (Ap. 4.1694ff). A night of black chaos.
20. **Medea's cauldron** (Apd. 1.9.27; Met. 7 ≈). A fake resurrection: Pelias' daughters are persuaded to boil their father.
21. **Caeneus** (Ap. 1.57ff ≈). An invulnerable man hammered into the earth alive.
22. **Kerostasia** (Il. 22.209 ≈). Death-fates weighed on golden scales: the burn lottery.
23. **Melissa's cold ghost** (Hdt. 5.92 ≈). A ghost complains she is cold; the city's clothes are burned in a pit, a rite at the Pyra.
24. **The Golden Fleece.**
    - Phrixus' ghost begs for his soul to be brought home (P. 4.159ff).
    - Its glow "gleamed like the lightning of Zeus" (Ap. 4.167ff).
    - Strabo's realist reading: gold was panned with fleeces (11.2.19).

## Funeral rites (the simulation's death pipeline)
1. **Prothesis**: the body lies in state.
2. **Ekphora**: the procession before sunrise.
3. **Pyra**: cremation, followed by **ostologia**, the gathering of the bones.
4. **Choai**: libations on the 3rd and 9th days.
5. **The circuit**: the dead's comrades circle the tomb three times in armour and hold games (Ap. 1.1057ff).
6. **The oar planted on the mound** (Od. 11.75 ≈).
7. **Charon's obol** in the mouth (Lucian *De Luctu* 10 ≈). This is the money sink.

**The unburied wander.** Patroclus could not pass the gates of Hades unburned (Il. 23.65ff ≈). Dead whose rites were skipped become restless **shades**, which drive horror events.

**Anthesteria** (≈). The flower festival when the dead walk the city for three days, ending with "Out the door, Kēres!" This is the Anthemoessans' holy day.

**Psychopomp.** Hermes leads the dead (Od. 24.1–14); here that office belongs to the Reapers' guild (Death cloaks).
